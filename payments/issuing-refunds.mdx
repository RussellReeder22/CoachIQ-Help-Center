You can give money back to a client in two ways, both from the client's profile:

| You want to… | Use | What happens |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Send money back to their card | **Refund** a payment | The money returns to the card or account they paid with |
| Take money off their next bill | **Invoice credit** | The amount comes off their next membership or purchase payment |

Neither one changes the client's access, memberships, bookings, or session credits. If a refund should also end a membership or remove credits, do that separately. See [Cancel a membership](/payments/manage-subscriptions) and [the client's Credits tab](/people/client-profiles/client-profiles-credits).

<Note>
  Refunds need an admin. Staff without access to the client's payments can't refund or see refund details.
</Note>

## Refund a payment

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open the client's payments">
    Go to **People**, open the client who paid, and open their **Transactions**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Choose the payment">
    Find the payment and select **Refund payment**. The refund window shows how much of the payment can still be refunded.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Enter the amount and reason">
    Leave the full amount, or enter less for a partial refund. Choose a reason. If you choose **Other**, add a note.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Confirm">
    Review the amount and confirm. The refund shows as pending, then as partly or fully refunded.
  </Step>
</Steps>

After the refund:

- The money goes back to the card or bank account the client paid with. Their bank usually shows it within 5–10 business days.
- The payer gets an emailed receipt.
- The payment's refund history shows the amount, status, reason, who refunded it, and when.
- Refunds show on the [Transactions page](/payments/transactions) and count in the **Refunds** card.

You can refund the rest of a partly refunded payment later, up to the original amount. While a refund is pending you can't submit another one for the same payment.

### When a payment can't be refunded here

- **A payment with an open dispute (chargeback):** respond to the dispute in Stripe instead.
- **Cash, check, or other payments you recorded yourself:** there's no card to send money back to. Return the money the way it was paid.
- **A failed refund:** the window says what went wrong. Check the payment in Stripe (select **Open** on the [Transactions page](/payments/transactions)) and try again.

### Refunds and Stripe fees

Stripe doesn't return its processing fee when you refund. On a \$100 card payment with a \$3.20 fee, a full refund gives the client \$100 back and you're out the \$3.20 fee. If your policy keeps the fee, refund the payment minus the fee as a partial refund, and say so in your refund policy before people buy.

### Refunding a membership payment

A refund returns the money for one payment only. The membership keeps billing on its schedule until it's canceled, and its credits stay. To stop future payments, [cancel the membership](/payments/manage-subscriptions).

To work out a prorated refund for part of a month: (payment ÷ days in the period) × days unused. For example, a \$200 monthly membership canceled halfway through gets \$100 back.

## Give invoice credit

Invoice credit is a dollar balance on the payer's account. Stripe takes it off their next bills automatically. Use it to make up for a missed session or fix a billing mistake without sending money back. It isn't a cash refund and it isn't a session credit.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open the client's invoice credits">
    Go to **People**, open the client, and open **Invoice Credits** in their payments. If the client has more than one payer, choose the payer.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Add the credit">
    Select **Add invoice credit**. Enter a positive amount in your organization's currency and a reason. The reason is for your staff only.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Review and confirm">
    Check the old balance, the credit, and the new balance, then confirm.
  </Step>
</Steps>

The credit is saved once, with a history entry showing the amount, reason, who added it, and when.

What the client sees:

- If they owe a balance, the credit pays that first.
- At checkout for a membership, the summary lists the plan, the account credit, and what's **Due today**. If the credit covers the whole first payment, checkout completes without a charge. A remainder under 50 cents is added to their next payment instead of being charged.
- In **Orders** and **Billing**, the first payment shows what they actually paid, for example "Initial payment \$15.00 (after \$5.00 credit)".

If the balance can't be loaded, the section says "Could not load invoice credits" with **Retry**. If Stripe isn't connected, it says "Invoice credits unavailable" and links to your Stripe settings. It never shows a balance it couldn't check as \$0.00.

## Refunds vs. chargebacks

A **refund** is you returning the money. A **chargeback** (dispute) is the client asking their bank to take it back. Disputes carry a Stripe fee even if you win, and you answer them in your Stripe Dashboard. When a client is unhappy and asks for their money back, a quick refund usually costs less than a dispute.
